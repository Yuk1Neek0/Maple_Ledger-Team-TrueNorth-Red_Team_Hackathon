Step one · The raw brief

What the brief asks for, in black and white

The problem: the Buy Canadian policy requires buying Canadian, but "Product of Canada / Made in Canada" today rests on supplier self-reporting — hard to verify, easy to fake. In multi-tier global supply chains, once a product changes hands a few times its origin cannot be reconstructed — a real risk for defence procurement.
The system the brief asks us to build
  • Establish provenance at the source, using cryptographic attestations from verified suppliers
  • Form a tamper-evident chain of custody across a multi-tier supply chain
  • Every supplier contribution → one signed attestation recording cost, labour, location
  • Attestations link across tiers → a complete, independently verifiable provenance record
  • Use it to compute whether a product qualifies as Made in Canada / Product of Canada
  • The drone sector as the primary use case

Does it qualify?

Made in Canada, Product of Canada, or neither.

A defensible number, not a yes/no guess.

End to end — by someone who trusts no one.

Supplier issuing experience — issue cryptographic attestations
B

All of the above is given by the brief — non-negotiable, must be answered. This is the bedrock.

Step two · Our reading

Beyond the literal brief — what we inferred

It's a "trust" problem, not a "tracking" problem
The brief keeps saying self-reported / easy to fake / hard to verify — not a logistics tracker
This is not a coding contest
3 of the 5 judging criteria are business / story / feasibility, plus a Best Presenter prize
Catching forgery is the hidden star deliverable
The event is called Red Team; the brief names tamper / forge / replay
Technology is not the moat
The brief is highly detailed — all 10 teams will converge; execution decides
Compute on "cost", not "value"
The brief says % of value, but the legal test is cost-based
Pure software · the drone is just a setting
All four deliverables are software experiences; no hardware
Use a hash-linked DAG, not a blockchain
A hash chain alone is tamper-evident; the brief never asks for consensus

Execution · Pre-build vs game day

Build the machine early; solve the problem on the day

What drops on 5/27 is "preparation material" — the specific challenge is only revealed at 9:15 on 30 May. So what can be done early and what must be done on the day need to be kept separate.

Pre-built · ~60–70%
  • The whole generic system, running on our own mock data
  • Frontend UI · crypto engine · data model + % algorithm
  • Mock data · 3 attack payloads · pitch deck
  • Start once the spec drops on 5/27
On the day · ≈5 hrs · must be on-site
  • Absorb the 9:15 briefing — the specific problem / data revealed
  • Swap in the real data, handle any curveball
  • The three parts integrated end-to-end for the first time
  • Demo against the real scenario · rehearse ×3 · present
Leave buffer. Expect a curveball on the day — pre-build with seams you can swap (data / scenario layer replaceable, never hard-code numbers); do not pack the 5 hours. The day is "land it + adapt + polish", not build from scratch.
