The situation
Canada just made Buy Canadian law.
But a defence supply chain you can’t verify is itself an attack surface.
A “Made in Canada” drone can carry a core chip from a foreign country — and that chip could be a backdoor. No tool today can catch it. In defence supply chains, counterfeit parts run as high as 15%.
The gap

The number the policy depends on is paper — filled in by the supplier

Buy Canadian now scores defence bids on a “Canadian-content %” — mandatory on contracts ≥ $25M, with the threshold dropping to $5M in spring 2026. That percentage is the bid score. It is money.
But today that number comes from a self-reported supplier form plus a manual spot-check.
Someone has every incentive to inflate it — and nothing can tell.
TrueNorth
What we do is simple —
Make that number provable.
TrueNorth issues a signed attestation for every contribution along the chain, and sums them into a Canadian-content % — one no one can alter, and anyone can recompute for themselves.

How it works

From a pile of parts, to a number you can prove

Every node carries its own cost and the Canadian portion inside it. Sum up the chain tier by tier and the drone’s Canadian % falls out — and because the graph mirrors the cost structure, any child that doesn’t reconcile with its parent is exposed at once.

✗ Hash mismatch · content no longer matches its signature

Forge
Inject a fake attestation signed with an unregistered key.
✗ Unknown issuer · does not chain to a trusted identity
ATTACK 03
Replay
Paste another drone’s genuine attestation onto this one.
✗ Replay detected · product binding mismatch
In all honesty Reconciliation catches a self-contradictory lie — but not one that is consistent across the entire chain. So we add three more layers — verified-identity binding · economic liability · sampled-audit anchoring. Stating the limits is itself defence-grade credibility.
